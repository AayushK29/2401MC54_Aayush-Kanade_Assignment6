Resource ordering prevents deadlock by requiring all processes to acquire shared resources in the same global order. In this experiment, two locks, Lock1 and Lock2, are used. Initially, Process A acquires Lock1 and waits for Lock2, while Process B acquires Lock2 and waits for Lock1, creating a circular wait and causing deadlock. A delay between acquisitions makes this condition reproducible. In the fixed version, both processes always acquire Lock1 before Lock2. Therefore, a circular wait cannot form. If one process holds Lock1, the other waits before acquiring Lock2. Once the first process releases both locks, the second proceeds. Thus, consistent resource ordering eliminates circular wait and prevents deadlock.
