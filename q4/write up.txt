In this experiment, five processes share three resource types: printers, scanners, and disks, each having a limited number of instances. Locks or semaphores are used to synchronize access to the resource pools. We use **resource ordering** as the deadlock-prevention strategy. All processes acquire required resources according to the same global order, preventing circular wait. Each process prints messages when requesting and acquiring resources, starting its work, and releasing them. The processes perform multiple cycles, ensuring that resources are never allocated beyond their available instances. Since all processes follow the same ordering, no circular wait can occur, and therefore no deadlock occurs. All five processes successfully complete their work.
